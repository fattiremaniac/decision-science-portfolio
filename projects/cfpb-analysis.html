CFPB Checking & Savings Complaint Analysis

An evidence-based investigation into an unusual January 2025 increase in consumer complaints—and what decision-makers should investigate next.

Guiding question: Given what the data tells us, what should the business do next?

137,016Unique complaint records analyzed
18,367Complaints received in January 2025
66.3%January complaints attributed to two institutions

Executive summary

January 2025 recorded 18,367 CFPB checking and savings complaints—approximately 283% more than December 2024. The increase was heavily concentrated in two institutions, but their reported issue categories were different.

Navy Federal Credit Union

7,411 January complaints. Of these, 6,970 (94.0%) were classified as Problem caused by your funds being low.

Capital One

4,759 January complaints. Of these, 4,474 (94.0%) were classified as Managing an account.

Analytical interpretation: A simultaneous spike does not necessarily indicate a common operational cause. The differing issue profiles point to distinct lines of investigation.

Recommended next step: Examine complaint narratives, submission patterns, and issue-level trends to determine whether the spike reflects operational problems, complaint-reporting effects, or another explanation.

Behind the analysis: technical methodology

  1. Define the question: Examine complaint volume and reported issues for checking and savings accounts during January 2024–December 2025.
  2. Prepare the data: Clean and deduplicate CFPB records, validate complaint IDs and dates, and establish a consistent analytical dataset of 137,016 unique records.
  3. Investigate with SQL and Python: Aggregate complaints by month, company, and issue; validate totals; and compare institution-specific patterns.
  4. Visualize findings: Export aggregated datasets and build a Tableau Public dashboard to communicate the trends.
  5. Translate evidence into action: Identify follow-up analyses that can test explanations rather than assume a cause.

Tools: Python, Pandas, SQL, SQLite, Jupyter Notebook, Tableau Public. Additional natural-language analysis is part of the broader research workflow, but its results are not presented as established findings on this page.

What should the business do next?

1. Investigate the two dominant issues

Review the relevant narratives and sub-issues separately for each institution, rather than treating the spikes as one phenomenon.

2. Check for reporting artifacts

Assess repeated narratives, submission timing, and changes in complaint patterns that could distort an apparent trend.

3. Test persistence

Compare the January spike with preceding and subsequent months to determine whether it represents a short-lived event or sustained change.

4. Validate before acting

Use corroborating evidence before attributing complaint counts to operational performance or recommending a specific remediation.

Data interpretation and limitations

CFPB complaint counts are not complaint rates and do not represent independently verified incidents. Institution size, customer populations, complaint-submission behavior, and data-publication patterns can affect comparisons.

These results identify patterns warranting further investigation; they do not establish the causes of the January 2025 spike or rank institutions by customer-service quality.
